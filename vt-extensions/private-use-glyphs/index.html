Private Use Glyphs#

Contour draws a few glyphs that Unicode has no character for. Rather than inventing a second drawing channel for them, they are given codepoints in a Private Use Area and rendered by the same built-in box-drawing renderer that draws U+2500–U+257F, the block elements and the legacy computing symbols.

This page documents those codepoints so that other terminals, fonts and tools can render the same glyphs if they want to. Nothing here is a protocol: no escape sequence introduces these characters, and Contour never requires an application to emit one.

The reserved block#

U+10F000–U+10F0FF — Contour Terminal private glyphs.

The block sits in Supplementary Private Use Area-B (plane 16) rather than in the BMP's U+E000–U+F8FF. That lower range is comprehensively claimed by Nerd Fonts — Powerline, Font Awesome, Devicons, Octicons, Seti, Material and others — and Contour already renders several of those assignments itself (U+E0B0–U+E0BE, U+EE00–U+EE05, U+F5D0–U+F60D).

It also sits away from the start of a plane. An allocation that outgrows the BMP tends to land at the beginning of the next area it is offered, which makes U+F0000 — the first codepoint of plane 15 — one of the more contended places to put a block, not one of the safer ones. U+10F000 is near the far end of plane 16 instead, where no widely-deployed assignment reaches.

Only the codepoints listed below are actually claimed by the renderer. The rest of the block is reserved for future use and deliberately falls through to the font, so that reserving a range costs nothing to anyone who is already using it.

Assigned codepoints#

Codepoint Name Glyph
U+10F000 FOLD HEAD OPEN A square box containing a minus, with a stem descending from its bottom edge to the bottom of the cell.
U+10F001 FOLD HEAD CLOSED A square box containing a plus. No stem.
U+10F002 BLOCK RUNNING A small filled triangle pointing right, as U+25B8 ▸.
U+10F003 USER MARK A filled diamond, as U+25C6 ◆.
U+10F004 BLOCK FINISHED A filled disc, as U+25CF ●.
U+10F005 BLOCK FAILED A diagonal cross, as U+2717 ✗.
U+10F006 FOLD BODY A vertical line through the cell, in the stem's column, as U+2502 │.
U+10F007 FOLD BODY END A vertical line from the cell's top to its middle, turning right to the cell's edge, as U+2514 └.
U+10F008 FOLD HEAD OPEN HEAVY U+10F000 drawn at twice the stroke.
U+10F009 FOLD HEAD CLOSED HEAVY U+10F001 drawn at twice the stroke.
U+10F00A FOLD BODY HEAVY U+10F006 drawn at twice the stroke.
U+10F00B FOLD BODY END HEAVY U+10F007 drawn at twice the stroke.
U+10F00C FOLD HEAD OPEN HEAVIER U+10F000 drawn at three times the stroke.
U+10F00D FOLD HEAD CLOSED HEAVIER U+10F001 drawn at three times the stroke.
U+10F00E FOLD BODY HEAVIER U+10F006 drawn at three times the stroke.
U+10F00F FOLD BODY END HEAVIER U+10F007 drawn at three times the stroke.
U+10F010 BLOCK SUCCEEDED A light check mark, as U+2713 ✓.
U+10F011 BLOCK FAILED DISC A filled disc with a diagonal cross knocked out of it: ✗ on ●.

The fold column is drawn as line art at the current foreground colour, scaled to the cell: the box is square whatever the cell's aspect ratio, and every glyph of the column shares one stroke and one column, so that the head's stem, the body and its end meet exactly. The stroke is the cell's own line weight -- twice it for the heavy four, three times for the heavier four -- even where the gutter draws its glyphs two cells wide.

The marks are filled shapes at the current foreground colour, anti-aliased, centred in the cell and spanning 60% of its shorter side, so they sit on the same line as the fold column's stem.

What they are for#

They form the head of Contour's fold column — the gutter drawn to the left of the grid, which marks each foldable OSC 133 command block:

⊟─ $ make -j8          <- U+10F000, the block is open
│    [  1/120] cc a.c  <- U+10F006
│    [  2/120] cc b.c
└    ... done          <- U+10F007, the block's last row

⊞  $ make -j8          <- U+10F001, the block is collapsed

The body and its end look like U+2502 (BOX DRAWINGS LIGHT VERTICAL) and U+2514 (BOX DRAWINGS LIGHT UP AND RIGHT), and once were. They are Contour's own because of the two-cell gutter: a box-drawing character drawn two cells wide takes the doubled stroke of a DECDWL row, whose text is stretched to twice its width, while the head beside it does not -- so the body stepped out of the stem's weight at every head. Drawn here, the column has one stroke in either width.

The head needs a codepoint of its own in any case. U+229F SQUARED MINUS and U+229E SQUARED PLUS are the box and its sign, but neither carries the stem that joins an open head to the body beneath it, and a head that did not connect would read as two unrelated marks rather than as one bracket down the side of a block.

A box carrying a plus or a minus, rather than a triangle, because that is the fold control a reader has already met in editors, tree views and file managers.

The marks share that column. A command that is still running heads its block with U+10F002, a finished one with no fold control beside it with U+10F004 -- or U+10F005 when it failed -- and a row the user marked (vi mm) carries U+10F003. A failed command whose output folds draws its whole fold heavy, head and body, with U+10F008 to U+10F00B, so failure reads by shape as well as by colour -- collapsed too, where only the head is drawn. The fold under the pointer is drawn one step heavier again (gutter.bold_on_hover): heavy for a light fold, U+10F00C to U+10F00F for a failed one.

The gutter's block column may be two cells wide (gutter.block_column_width); its glyphs are then drawn across both cells, each twice the size.

The native chromes draw the block column as a rail instead: a bar in a strip of pixels between the gutter's cells and the grid, which marks a block's head with U+10F002 while it runs, with U+10F011 when it failed -- one glyph, so the cross is the page showing through the disc -- and with U+10F010, only while the block is hovered or focused, when it succeeded. A row the user marked carries U+10F003 there too. The rail's marks are drawn two cells wide, sized to the row; the Terminal chrome keeps the glyph column above.

Unicode has these four shapes at U+25B8, U+25C6, U+25CF and U+2717 (and the check at U+2713), but the gutter does not use them: the renderer's claim on a codepoint is global, so claiming those would draw every one a program writes -- the ● of systemctl status, a prompt theme's ▸ or ◆, DEC Special Graphics' ` -- as a gutter mark instead of in its font's own size and weight.

Notes for implementers#

  • These characters are produced by the terminal, for its own gutter. They are not part of any escape sequence, and an application has no reason to write one.
  • A Private Use Area assignment is by definition not exclusive. Planes 15 and 16 are also used by some East Asian fonts for user-defined characters (GB 18030 and Big5 user-defined areas), so a document that already uses U+10F000 for something else will render as a fold head in Contour. Turning the gutter off (folding.show_markers: false) does not change that, because the glyphs are claimed by the renderer rather than by the fold feature — if this ever proves to matter in practice, the claim is one entry in BoxDrawingRenderer::renderable().
  • If you implement these in a font, note that Contour will still prefer its own rendition: the box-drawing renderer is consulted before font shaping, and font.builtin_box_drawing: false turns that off for all built-in glyphs, not just these.
